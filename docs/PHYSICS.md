# Physics implementation notes

## Design and references

The existing simulation owns product flow, machine cycles, signals, robots and navigation. A [cannon-es](https://github.com/pmndrs/cannon-es) rigid-body world runs at the same fixed 1/60 s step and supplies gravity, mass, contacts, friction and sleep without replacing those systems. The synchronous JavaScript solver fits the existing synchronous engine and offline single-file build. Cannon's [body API](https://pmndrs.github.io/cannon-es/docs/) documents its mass and sleeping model. The collider design was also informed by [Rapier's collider guidance](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/colliders/): dynamic models require suitable convex/compound shapes rather than assuming arbitrary animated meshes are stable colliders. Rapier itself is not included.

## Simulation ownership and collisions

- Authored item transforms and physics mode/mass are saved; runtime poses are ephemeral. The viewport, selection gizmos and moving signal wires read those poses. Reset reconstructs bodies at authored poses. Kinematic equipment holds its edited pose; task-driven workers still walk and their kinematic colliders follow.
- Most scene components use oriented boxes or compounds (platforms, stairs, feeder frames and conveyor segments). Imported meshes instead produce a **surface-voxel compound** from their actual triangles, including mesh transforms. Adjacent cells merge into boxes, and the grid coarsens when needed to keep the compound at or below 128 shapes. Large openings can remain open, unlike a single bounding box. Scene imports and imported workpieces share the same axis, unit, centering and scale conversions as the renderer. Ordinary-speed workpiece contacts and stacking support also use those imported patches. Older projects lacking bounds or collision data generate it in the background, even for hidden or deleted sources; a bounding box is the temporary fallback until then.
- The voxel compound is still an approximation, not exact triangle-mesh or deformable/animated collision. Fine holes, curved surfaces and intricate geometry may be coarsened. Built-in machinery and robot arms use simplified collision geometry; arms and mechanisms remain animation-driven, not articulated rigid bodies. Unusual stacking, extremely steep or overhanging belts and complex contacts remain approximate.
- For fast motion, a custom **swept oriented-box** pass checks translating compound shapes using continuous separating-axis tests and relative motion, and clamps to the earliest time of impact. It covers dynamic bodies, the floor, kinematic components and free products, including products in otherwise legacy scenes and fast product/product pairs. Rotational movement is split into short intervals and conservatively inflated; a potential rotational hit stops the spinning participant at a safe pose. This builds on the distinction between substeps and time-of-impact sweeps described in [Box2D's continuous collision notes](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_ContinuousCollision_GDC2013.pdf) and [Bepu's CCD documentation](https://github.com/bepu/bepuphysics2/blob/master/Documentation/ContinuousCollisionDetection.md).
- The CCD pass supplements Cannon rather than being native Cannon CCD. Initially embedded shapes are left to the discrete solver; angular casts are conservative and can stop early. Affected bodies are clamped at impact rather than advancing their full remaining motion in that exceptional step. Arbitrary deforming meshes, exact torque at the swept contact, simultaneous dense impacts and all possible speeds are not guaranteed. No industrial safety or precision is implied.
- The conveyor/stack integrator still owns product motion. Dynamic belts and rollers use a dedicated frictionless **workpiece contact pair** so stationary-surface solver friction does not cancel the existing belt motor. Other equipment/workpiece pairs and belt/equipment pairs retain friction. This follows Cannon's [contact-material rules](https://pmndrs.github.io/cannon-es/docs/classes/Material.html); tangential belt motion is still driven by the specialized integrator, not a physical conveyor motor in Cannon.
- Legacy scenes keep their lightweight normal-speed path; choosing kinematic/dynamic opts a component into ordinary physical contacts. A fast product temporarily activates the swept path even in a legacy scene. Product support on tilted equipment uses oriented faces, while conveyor support uses a sampled 3D spline and vertical layer-aware lookup.

## Workers and imported workpieces

When platforms or stairs exist, a height-layered A* search distinguishes overlapping floors, links adjacent reachable stair treads, checks a pedestrian footprint near upper edges and filters barriers/other people by height. Kinematic and dynamic workers use the route; the dynamic path has a bounded stair step-up motor. Patrol waypoint Y is relative to the worker's starting height; if heights are left automatic, the highest surface at the waypoint is selected. Station and raised-conveyor endpoint elevations select the intended floor. A disconnected destination leaves the worker waiting rather than routing across a ledge. Routes are sampled on a 0.25 m grid with a bounded search and a roughly 0.26 m step limit, so narrow, poorly aligned or unusually steep connections may not be traversable; arbitrary imported meshes are not detailed navigation obstacles. Dynamic equipment refreshes spatial indices, and a blocked route is replanned instead of invalidating every worker's path for every remote movement.

Each imported model also has a saved workpiece definition, separate from its scene instance. It remains selectable and rendered after the source object is deleted, and survives undo/redo, autosave and project export/reload. Source assets are deduplicated in the project. The saved definition can be explicitly removed from a product picker, with confirmation and undo, to recover project space. Its configured mass is used for payload checks. Definitions are not inserted as visible/physical scene bodies.

For CPU cost, level belts retain a lightweight support path, long conveyor compounds have a bounded shape count, and Cannon's sweep-and-prune broadphase and sleeping are enabled. Operational indices normally refresh every four simulation steps while equipment is moving and once at sleep. A dynamic belt or roller carrying a workpiece refreshes sooner when its translation or rotation at the furthest carried part exceeds about 3 cm; other moving supports can still show a short indexing lag. Ordinary static wires reuse geometry, whereas moving wires are rebuilt at most 20 times per second. Default weights are illustrative, not measured masses.

## Validation and benchmark

`npm run test:unit` includes swept impacts at 20–10,000 m/s, rotated walls and rotating compounds, moving targets, falling and crossing products, near misses, imported openings/stacking, stair round-trips on overlapping and three-storey floors, disconnected routes, and cross-floor carry/tend workflows. It also retains the slope, belt, friction, gravity, sleeping, signal and robot regressions. With a development server running, `npm run test:physics-browser` covers the height gizmo, patrol controls, an imported STL, deleted-source undo/redo, cleanup, autosave reload and background measurement of legacy hidden workpieces, dynamic rendering and moving wires. After `npm run build`, `npm run test:physics-standalone` checks the packaged HTML with network requests blocked.

`npm run bench:physics` is a headless CPU micro-benchmark, not a browser FPS measurement. On the sandbox's Node 22 / Intel Xeon 2.60 GHz, one run with 25/100/250 bodies measured falling means of 1.16/0.96/1.64 ms and settled means of 0.05/0.06/0.28 ms per fixed step, with all bodies asleep at the end. Results vary by hardware and scene; the CCD work and large imported compounds can add cost when active.
