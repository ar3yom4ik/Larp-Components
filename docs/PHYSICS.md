# Physics implementation notes

## Design and references

The existing simulation owns product flow, machine cycles, signals, robots and navigation. The new [cannon-es](https://github.com/pmndrs/cannon-es) rigid-body world runs at the same fixed 1/60 s step and supplies gravity, mass, contacts, friction and sleep without replacing those systems. We chose the synchronous pure-JavaScript solver to fit the existing synchronous engine and offline single-file build. Cannon's [world/step and body API](https://pmndrs.github.io/cannon-es/docs/) documents the stepping, broadphase, mass and sleeping model.

The collider design was also informed by [Rapier's rigid-body guidance](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies/) and [collider guidance](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/colliders/): treat dynamic and kinematic bodies differently, use oriented primitives/compound shapes for movable bodies, and avoid assuming an arbitrary animated mesh is an accurate dynamic collider. Rapier itself is not included. Continuous collision detection, articulated robot dynamics and mesh-exact contact are not implemented here.

## Simulation ownership

- Authored item transforms and physics mode/mass are saved in the project; runtime dynamic poses are ephemeral. The viewport, selection gizmos and moving signal wires read these poses. Reset reconstructs bodies at authored poses.
- Dynamic scene items use simple oriented boxes or compound boxes (such as platforms, stairs, feeder frames and conveyor segments). Kinematic equipment is fixed at its authored position; task-driven workers still walk and their kinematic proxies follow. Ordinary old scenes without dynamic or tilted/explicitly kinematic components keep the previous lightweight path. Once physics is active, scene proxies collide with dynamic bodies; dynamic and explicitly kinematic/tilted non-belt proxies also exchange contact impulses with free products. Conveyor product motion remains handled by the specialized belt/stack integrator, not a simulated conveyor friction surface.
- Dynamic conveyor and roller bodies use a dedicated, frictionless **workpiece contact pair** while retaining normal collision forces; otherwise stationary-surface solver friction cancels the belt's existing motor. Other equipment/workpiece pairs and belt/equipment pairs retain friction. This follows Cannon's [contact material rules](https://pmndrs.github.io/cannon-es/docs/classes/Material.html): per-material friction overrides the pair, so the belt material leaves it unspecified. Tangential belt motion is still driven by the existing integrator, consistent with the [moving-surface contact approach](https://pybullet.org/Bullet/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?t=6730), rather than claiming a physical conveyor motor in Cannon.
- Product support on tilted equipment is computed from oriented faces; conveyor support uses a sampled 3D spline and vertical layer-aware lookup. Extremely steep/overhanging belts, complex contacts and fast bodies are approximate. The fixed time step does not prevent tunnelling through thin shapes; continuous collision detection is not available in this integration (see the [Cannon-es CCD discussion](https://github.com/pmndrs/cannon-es/discussions)).
- Workers keep their navigation/task logic. A bounded step-up motor allows dynamic workers to climb ordinary stair treads; the non-dynamic path follows nearby tread/deck heights. Routing is still planar: it does not choose stair connections between floors or avoid unguarded ledges.
- Imported source geometry is rendered as the workpiece, but collision uses its source bounding box adjusted for units, up-axis and scale. Its configured mass is used for robot payload checks. The source scene item remains the product definition and must not be deleted; it can be hidden.

For CPU cost, level belts retain a lightweight support path, long conveyor compound colliders have a bounded shape count, and Cannon's sweep-and-prune broadphase and sleeping are enabled; settled bodies stop triggering expensive runtime scene/index rebuilds. Operational indices normally refresh every four simulation steps while equipment is moving and once at sleep. A dynamic belt or roller carrying a workpiece refreshes sooner when its translation or rotation at the furthest carried part exceeds about 3 cm, so fast falling/rotating supports don't visibly outrun their products; other moving supports can still show a short indexing lag. Ordinary static wires reuse geometry, whereas moving wires are rebuilt at most 20 times per second. Defaults are illustrative, not measured real-world masses. Small contacts and solver timing can differ across devices and extremely dense scenes.

## Validation and benchmark

`npm run test:unit` includes 3D slopes, crossing layers, stoppers, tilted supports/walls, gravity, sleeping/wake, mass and dynamic collisions, powered/inactive/falling/rotating dynamic belts and rollers, contact-pair friction, stairs, dynamic robot/feeder signals and imported assets. With a development server running, `npm run test:physics-browser` covers the height gizmo, a real STL workpiece, dynamic rendering, moving cables/ports and the component catalog. After `npm run build`, `npm run test:physics-standalone` checks the packaged HTML with network requests blocked. `npm run bench:physics` measures the CPU time for a fixed simulation step with falling and settled bodies; it does not measure browser FPS. Results depend on the machine and are not guarantees. On the sandbox's Node 22 / Intel Xeon 2.60 GHz, one run with 25/100/250 bodies measured falling means of 1.37/1.38/1.93 ms and settled means of 0.03/0.06/0.29 ms per fixed step (all bodies asleep at the end). Three-trial legacy `Engine.step` medians against `88a60d3` ranged from about 0.1% to 2.1% slower across four scenarios; these are noisy CPU micro-benchmarks, not rendering or FPS results.
